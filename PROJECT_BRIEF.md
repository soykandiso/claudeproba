## 1. Operating context

I am a solo founder based in Skopje, North Macedonia. Background: financial analysis in banking and payments, subsidy and grant consulting for companies, certified court translator (Macedonian–Turkish), Python/Flask developer. I work on this in evenings and weekends.

Every architectural decision must optimise for **one person being able to operate, debug and extend this system for years**. Boring, well-documented technology beats clever technology. If a choice adds a moving part, it must justify itself against that standard.

## 2. Product definition

**One-liner:** A company or individual enters a short profile; the platform returns the funding programmes they can realistically apply for, explains why, and then sells the preparation of the application documents.

**User journey:**

1. **Profile intake** (target: under 3 minutes). Entity type, sector/NACE, municipality, year founded, headcount, turnover band, ownership characteristics, planned investment type and size, timeline, keywords describing the project.
2. **Instant shortlist** (target: under 5 seconds). Deterministic query against the registry of currently open calls. Free, no account required beyond an email.
3. **Deep analysis** (target: under 3 minutes). Retrieval over the official text of the top candidates, clause-level eligibility verification, a written report with citations, gaps, required documents, deadlines and a realistic assessment of fit. Paid.
4. **Document package.** Preparation of the application set for a chosen call: forms, project description, budget tables, business plan sections, annexes checklist, in the required language. Paid, higher tier.
5. **Monitoring subscription.** Ongoing alerts when new calls match the saved profile. Recurring revenue.

**Explicitly out of scope for v1:** automatic submission to any authority, any guarantee of award, legal representation, accounting services.

## 3. Non-negotiable constraints

### 3.1 Jurisdiction and language
- Primary market: North Macedonia. Interface and outputs in **Macedonian (Cyrillic), Albanian, and English**. Turkish is a later addition.
- Dates `dd.mm.yyyy`. Currency `МКД` and `EUR`, never assume USD.
- Macedonian text must be correct Cyrillic with proper terminology, not machine-mangled. Any AI-produced Macedonian passes a review gate before reaching a customer.

### 3.2 Payments
- **Stripe does not operate in North Macedonia. PayPal accounts here cannot receive money.** Do not design around either.
- v1 billing: proforma invoice plus bank transfer in denars, generated by the system, reconciled manually. This is how B2B already works locally and it removes all platform risk.
- Design a `PaymentProvider` interface with an `InvoiceProvider` implementation first, so a local card gateway (CPay/CaSys via a merchant agreement) or Payoneer can be added later without touching business logic.

### 3.3 Data protection
- Personal and company data is collected. Macedonian data protection law is GDPR-aligned; treat GDPR as the baseline.
- **Minimise what reaches any third-party LLM API.** Strip or pseudonymise names, EMBS/EDB numbers, addresses and contact details before prompt construction. The model needs the *shape* of the applicant, not their identity.
- Maintain: consent capture, retention policy, deletion on request, a record of processing, and a subprocessor list including model providers.
- Store the raw source snapshots of official calls, not just parsed output, for audit.

### 3.4 The accuracy contract (most important section in this document)
- **Deterministic rules decide eligibility. The model never does.** Hard criteria (entity type, sector, region, company age, size thresholds, deadline) are SQL filters over structured fields.
- The model's only jobs are: extracting structured fields from official text (schema-validated, rejected on failure), verifying nuanced criteria against retrieved passages, and writing explanations.
- **Every eligibility statement shown to a user must carry a citation**: source document, section, retrieval date, and a link. No citation, no claim.
- Output uses graded confidence: `Eligible` / `Likely eligible` / `Needs verification` / `Not eligible`, each with reasons. The words "guaranteed", "approved" and "you will receive" never appear.
- Any call whose parsed data fails validation goes to a human review queue instead of being published.

### 3.5 Cost and scale
- Infrastructure under €15/month until there are 20 paying customers. Model routing: cheapest tier for extraction and classification, stronger models only for customer-facing prose.
- Assume tens of thousands of documents, not millions. Do not build for scale that will not arrive.

## 4. Domain model (draft — challenge it)

- `Applicant` — profile, owner account, language preference, consent records
- `Programme` — a durable funding instrument (e.g. IPARD Measure 1)
- `Call` — a dated instance of a programme with a deadline, budget and document set
- `EligibilityCriterion` — structured, machine-evaluable rule attached to a Call, with `source_quote`, `source_url`, `confidence`, `extracted_at`
- `MatchResult` — Applicant × Call, with score, verdict, per-criterion outcomes
- `Evidence` — retrieved passage backing a specific statement
- `DocumentPackage`, `DocumentTemplate`, `GeneratedDocument`
- `Order`, `Invoice`, `Subscription`
- `SourceFeed`, `IngestionRun`, `RawSnapshot` (content hash, fetched_at, http status)
- `ReviewQueueItem` — anything requiring human sign-off

Challenge this model. In particular, argue for or against separating `Programme` from `Call`, and propose how to represent criteria that are genuinely unstructurable.

## 5. Data sources

Start with **six sources**, prove the pipeline end to end, then expand. Do not attempt breadth first.

**Domestic candidates:**
- Фонд за иновации и технолошки развој (FITR)
- Агенција за финансиска поддршка во земјоделството и руралниот развој (IPARD / national measures)
- Министерство за економија
- Агенција за вработување на РСМ (employment subsidies)
- Развојна банка на Северна Македонија
- Град Скопје and larger municipalities
- data.gov.mk open datasets where available

**International candidates:**
- EU Funding & Tenders Portal (Horizon Europe, Single Market Programme, IPA III) — check for an official API before scraping
- Erasmus+ and Creative Europe
- EBRD / EIB / WB EDIF instruments for the Western Balkans
- Bilateral donor programmes active in North Macedonia (UNDP, GIZ, Swiss, Nordic)

**Ingestion rules:**
- Prefer official API or RSS over HTML scraping. Respect `robots.txt`, terms of use and sane rate limits. Identify the crawler honestly with contact details.
- Content-hash every fetch; only spend model tokens on changed documents.
- Every source gets a health check and an alert when it has returned nothing new for longer than its expected cadence. A silently dead scraper is the primary failure mode of this business.
- Record `last_verified_at` per call and surface staleness in the UI.

## 6. Matching engine specification

Four stages, in order:

1. **Hard filter (SQL).** Removes everything the applicant cannot legally apply for. Fast, cheap, auditable.
2. **Score and rank.** Weighted fit on sector proximity, investment size against call budget bands, timeline against deadline, past-awardee similarity if data allows. Deterministic, testable.
3. **Verification pass (retrieval + model).** For the top N only. Retrieve the relevant clauses of the official text and check the nuanced criteria. Output is structured JSON with a verdict and a citation per criterion, schema-validated.
4. **Human review gate.** For the first months, every paid report is approved by me before delivery. Design the queue as a first-class feature, not a workaround.

Latency targets: stage 1–2 under 5 seconds (this is the "instant" experience); stages 3–4 asynchronous with email notification, minutes not hours.

Propose an evaluation harness: a fixed set of applicant profiles with known-correct answers, run on every change to the matching logic. Regressions in this suite block deployment.

## 7. AI usage rules

- All model calls go through a single internal service with: model routing by task, strict JSON schema validation, retry-then-fail (never publish unvalidated output), token and cost logging per feature, and full prompt/response audit storage.
- Retrieval-augmented by default. The model answers from retrieved official text, not from its own knowledge of grant programmes, which will be outdated and wrong.
- Aggressive caching keyed on content hash. Re-analysing an unchanged call is pure waste.
- Any customer-facing document produced with model assistance is labelled as AI-assisted and human-reviewed.
- Prompts are versioned files in the repository, not strings buried in code.

---

## 8. Document generation tier

- Templates per programme, maintained as version-controlled files, producing `.docx` and `.pdf` with correct Macedonian formatting.
- Generated content is a **draft for human completion**, and the UI must say so. Financial projections and legal declarations are never auto-filled with invented figures; they are marked as fields requiring the applicant's real data.
- Every package ships with a checklist of what the applicant must still provide (signatures, certificates, bank confirmations).
- Never submit anything to an authority on the user's behalf in v1.

## 9. Proposed stack (challenge every line)

- Python 3.12, FastAPI or Flask (recommend and justify one; I know Flask)
- PostgreSQL with `pgvector`, SQLAlchemy, Alembic migrations
- Background jobs: RQ or APScheduler; argue for the simpler one that survives solo operation
- Server-rendered Jinja2 with HTMX and Tailwind. No SPA unless you can defend the maintenance cost
- Playwright only where static fetching fails
- `docxtpl` / WeasyPrint for document output
- Docker Compose, Caddy for TLS, single small VPS, Cloudflare in front
- Automated nightly database dumps to off-site object storage, with a documented and *tested* restore procedure

## 10. Delivery phases

Each phase ends with a demonstrable, acceptance-tested increment.

- **P0 — Plan.** Deliverables in section 13. No code.
- **P1 — Ingestion.** Six sources flowing into the registry, with snapshots, change detection, health checks and a review queue. Acceptance: a new call published by FITR appears in the database within 24 hours, with a snapshot and citations.
- **P2 — Matching.** Profile intake, hard filters, scoring, verification pass, evaluation harness. Acceptance: ten test profiles produce correct shortlists, every criterion cited.
- **P3 — Public surface.** Landing page, multilingual UI, public SEO archive of closed calls, email capture. Acceptance: indexed archive pages, working signup.
- **P4 — Money.** Paid deep report, invoice generation, manual reconciliation, delivery. Acceptance: end-to-end from order to delivered PDF report with an issued invoice.
- **P5 — Document packages.** Templates for the two highest-volume programmes. Acceptance: a complete draft package produced and human-reviewed for a real call.
- **P6 — Monitoring subscription.** Saved profiles, alerts, recurring invoicing.

## 11. Risk register to address in the plan

For each: likelihood, impact, mitigation, and the earliest cheap test that would reveal it.

- A source changes layout or blocks the crawler and matches silently go stale
- The model reports a company as eligible when it is not, and a customer wastes real money
- Willingness to pay is lower than assumed in the Macedonian SME market
- Calls are seasonal, so demand is spiky and revenue is lumpy
- Personal data exposure through model API calls
- Liability framing: this is information and drafting assistance, not legal, tax or financial advice, and the terms must say so plainly
- Single-operator bus factor: what happens if I am unavailable for two weeks
- Competition from the ministries themselves publishing a better portal
